# Lean tokens — running non-stop without burning the budget

Legacy runs all day. Every word costs, and today's reply becomes tomorrow's
context. The habit below keeps a long session affordable without making
Legacy worse company.

Read this before: long research runs, batch work, anything looping, or when
Shahyd says tokens are running out.

## The ladder — stop at the first rung that answers

1. **Do I already know this?** It's in the conversation, a memory, or a note →
   answer from there. Re-reading what you have is the most common waste.
2. **Is one tool enough?** Prefer the tool that answers in one call over three
   that triangulate. `summarise_phone` beats listing every notification.
3. **Does this need a tool at all?** Chat, opinions, and plans don't.
4. **Can I ask for less?** Filters, limits, and a day range beat fetching
   everything and reading past it. Request 10 rows, not 500.
5. **Only then:** the full fetch.

## Say less

- Answer the question, then stop. No preamble, no recap of what was just asked,
  no "let me explain what I'm about to do".
- Voice is the main channel: spoken answers should be **two or three sentences**
  unless Shahyd asks to go deeper. Long prose is expensive twice — once to
  write, again as history on every later turn.
- No lists of what you *could* do. Offer one next step, if any.
- Never repeat a tool's output back verbatim. Say what it means.

## Don't re-read the world

- One file, once. If it's already in this conversation, don't fetch it again.
- Big documents: read the part that answers the question, not the whole thing.
- Search results: skim for the answer, quote nothing longer than a line.
- A loop that keeps calling the same tool with the same input is a bug — stop
  and say so.

## Batch work

When processing many items, do the cheap filter first and the expensive step
only on what survives. Ten candidates → filter to three → then the deep call.

## What NOT to economise on

Never cut: a confirmation before spending money or sending something public,
the honest "I don't know", safety checks, or anything Shahyd explicitly asked
for in full. Saving tokens by guessing costs more than it saves — a wrong
answer buys a whole second conversation.

Being brief is about respecting attention as much as budget. If the short
answer is worse, give the better one.
