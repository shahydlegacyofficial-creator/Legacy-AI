# Proposals that win the work

Read this before writing any proposal, quote or pitch document. The tool that
builds the file is `create_proposal`; this is what to put in it.

## The thing most proposals get wrong

They describe the seller. Shahyd's credits, the studio's gear, the years of
experience. All true, all his, and none of it is what the client is deciding
about.

**A client is deciding whether their problem goes away.** Everything in a
proposal earns its place by moving them toward believing it will.

## The shape

**1. What they need, in their words.** Open by stating their problem back to
them more clearly than they stated it. If that paragraph is right, they read
the rest already trusting you. If it is generic, they skim.

Not "you require music for your film." Rather: "you have ninety minutes that
has to carry three tonal shifts, a delivery date that does not move, and a
trailer cut that lands before the film does."

**2. What you will do about it.** Concrete and countable. Cues, stems, revisions,
formats, who attends what. A client cannot compare "a full score" against
another quote; they can compare "22 cues, 7.1 and stereo stems, two rounds of
revisions per cue."

**3. When.** Real dates, with the dependencies named — "final mixes 30 August,
assuming picture lock by the 8th." A date with its condition attached is a
professional's date. A date without one is a hostage.

**4. What it costs, and what it does not include.** Say the number plainly and
early enough that they are not hunting for it. Then say what falls outside it.
Naming exclusions up front reads as confidence; discovering them later reads as
a trap, and that is how good relationships end.

**5. Why him.** LAST, and short. Two or three specifics that bear on THIS job —
7.1 cinema delivery if it is a theatrical release, the Dhivehi-language work if
it is local, the turnaround on a previous trailer if speed is their worry.
Relevance, not a résumé.

## Rules

**Never invent a credit, a number, or a capability.** If you do not know
whether he has done something, ask him. A proposal is a document a client may
hold him to.

**Price is a decision, not a guess.** Use `studio_rates`. If the job does not
fit the rate card, say so and give him the reasoning rather than inventing a
figure — the number is his to choose.

**One page of substance beats five of padding.** A busy client reads the first
paragraph and the price. Everything else is for the person they have to justify
it to.

**Write it as him.** He is a working composer with real credits — plain,
confident, no adjectives doing the work of facts. No "passionate", no "we are
excited to", no "sonic journey".

## Before it goes out

- Every number checked against something real
- Every date has its dependency stated
- The exclusions are visible, not buried
- Nothing claimed that he has not actually done
- It would still make sense read aloud in thirty seconds

Then show him. **A proposal is his name on a page — he sends it, not you.**
