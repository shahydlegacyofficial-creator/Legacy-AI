# Marketing

Read this first. It tells you which playbook the task actually needs, so you
read one, not six.

## Which playbook

| The task | Read |
|---|---|
| A caption, a headline, a sales page, an opt-in page, a sales letter | `marketing-copy` |
| An email, a sequence, a broadcast, a post-purchase follow-up | `marketing-email` |
| A paid ad, a lead magnet, an opt-in offer | `marketing-ads` |
| A content plan, a posting calendar, what to measure | `marketing-content` |
| Where a piece fits, or building the whole funnel | `marketing-funnel` |
| Anything, when you want the underlying thinking | `marketing-principles` |

**Read ONE.** They overlap on purpose so each stands alone. Reading three
because the task touches three is how a caption ends up costing more than the
campaign.

For a short caption or a one-line hook, the rules below are usually enough on
their own — go straight to writing.

## The rules that always apply

**Sell the feeling, not the product.** Nobody wants a film score; they want
their film to land. Nobody wants a resort transfer; they want the holiday to
start the moment they arrive.

**The headline is most of the job.** If the first line does not earn the second,
nothing after it matters. Write ten and throw away nine.

**Write to one person.** "Producers who need a score delivered on time" is a
category. Write to the one producer, at his desk, with a lock date in three
weeks.

**Specific beats clever.** "22 cues, delivered in 7.1, two rounds of revisions"
outsells "world-class sonic storytelling" every time, and it has the advantage
of being true.

**One thing to do next.** Every piece ends with exactly one action. Two choices
is the same as none.

## For Shahyd specifically

Three businesses that do not share an audience:

- **Legacy Studios** — the buyer is a producer or director with a deadline. Not
  browsing; looking for someone who will not make the film late. Lead with
  reliability and real credits.
- **Travel Essence** — Shana runs it. A slow, emotional, comparison-heavy
  decision. Feeling first, price late.
- **The masterclass and merch** — they already know who he is. Do not sell to
  them like strangers; restating who he is wastes the message.

**Never invent a number, a credit or a testimonial.** Read `studio_rates` and
the client list instead. A made-up claim is one he has to defend in person, on
a small island where everyone knows everyone.

**He signs it, not you.** Marketing goes out under his name — draft it, show
him, let him send it.

**Local audience:** mostly Maldivian and bilingual. English is fine; forced
Dhivehi transliteration reads as trying too hard. Prayer times shape when
people are on their phones — schedule around them.
